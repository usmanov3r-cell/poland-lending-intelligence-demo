SYNTHETIC - FICTIONAL LENDERS, HYPOTHETICAL RATE CUT

A competitor cut its fee. Should we copy it?

Instalment loan, PLN 8,000 / 12 months, new customers. We compare two options in the same month, so the rate cut affects both equally.

1. Why this matters in Poland

Polish law caps the price of consumer loans in two ways:

  • Interest - at most 2 x (NBP reference rate + 3.5 pp). When the NBP cuts its rate, the cap falls for every lender right away.
  • Fees and other non-interest costs - at most 10% of the loan + 10% per year, never above 45% (Art. 36a); here PLN 1,600.00. Not linked to the NBP rate.

Most non-bank lenders already price at or near these caps, so a rate cut lowers their income without any decision on their part.

2. What happened

NBP reference rate (hypothetical)3.75% → 3.50% Interest cap 14.50% → 14.00%. Interest on the loan at the cap: PLN 642.16 → 619.56 (- PLN 22.60 per loan, for everyone).
Competitor X (fictional)Fee PLN 1,400.00 → 1,250.00 On top of the lower interest, it cut its fee by PLN 150.00 to win customers. Customer pays PLN 1,869.56 instead of our PLN 2,019.56.

The question: keep our fee at PLN 1,400.00, or match the competitor at PLN 1,250.00?

5. When would the answer change?

Copying wins if take-up rises above40.7%We assumed 38%. Will a PLN 150.00 lower fee bring that many more customers?
Copying also wins if keeping our fee drops take-up below32.7%Today 35%. Watch our own take-up after the competitor's move.

Stress tests

  • The 30 extra customers are riskier (PD 15% instead of 10%): copying loses PLN 31,513.20.
  • Keeping our fee loses some customers (take-up 33%): keeping still earns PLN 3,272.00 more.

6. Decision

Don't copy automatically. Test the lower fee on a share of new applications and measure take-up and early defaults.

  • Copy only if take-up rises above 40.7% and the extra customers do not default more.
  • Reconsider if our own take-up falls below 32.7% while we keep the fee.

Not modelled

  • Early repayment fee refunds (Art. 49) - reduce fee income in both options.
  • Repeat loans - more new customers may pay off later; the strongest argument for copying.
  • Discounting, capital and tax.
